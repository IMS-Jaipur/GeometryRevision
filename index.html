Lines, Angles & Triangles

1.1Lines & angles

  • Angle types: acute \((\lt90^\circ)\), right \((90^\circ)\), obtuse \((90^\circ\text{ to }180^\circ)\), straight \((180^\circ)\), reflex \((180^\circ\text{ to }360^\circ)\).
  • Complementary angles add to \(90^\circ\); supplementary angles add to \(180^\circ\).
  • Angles on a straight line (a linear pair) add to \(180^\circ\). Vertically opposite angles are equal. Angles round a point add to \(360^\circ\).
Formula · Two parallel lines cut by a transversal

Corresponding angles are equal. Alternate interior angles are equal. Alternate exterior angles are equal.

Co-interior angles (same side of the transversal, between the lines) add to \(180^\circ\).

1.2Angle & side fundamentals

  • Sum of interior angles \(=180^\circ\). Sum of exterior angles \(=360^\circ\).
  • Exterior angle \(=\) sum of the two remote interior angles.
  • Triangle inequality: each side is less than the sum and more than the difference of the other two: \(|b-c|\lt a\lt b+c\).
  • Side–angle correspondence: the largest side lies opposite the largest angle (and vice versa). Equal sides \(\Leftrightarrow\) equal opposite angles.
  • Every side is shorter than the semi-perimeter \(s\).
  • Type from the sides, with \(c\) the longest: \(c^2\lt a^2+b^2\) acute, \(c^2=a^2+b^2\) right, \(c^2\gt a^2+b^2\) obtuse.
  • Isosceles triangle: the altitude from the apex is also the median, the angle bisector and the perpendicular bisector of the base.
Topper trick · Counting the third side

With two sides \(p\ge q\) fixed, an integer third side \(x\) satisfies \(p-q\lt x\lt p+q\), which gives \(2q-1\) values.

For obtuse triangles, count both cases: \(x\) the longest with \(x^2\gt p^2+q^2\), or \(p\) the longest with \(x^2\lt p^2-q^2\). Sides 8 and 15 give \(18\le x\le22\) and \(8\le x\le12\): 10 triangles.

Formula · Integer-sided triangles of perimeter n

Number of triangles = nearest integer to \(\dfrac{n^2}{48}\) if \(n\) is even, or to \(\dfrac{(n+3)^2}{48}\) if \(n\) is odd.

1.3Area, all the ways

Formula · Area of a triangle
\[\Delta=\tfrac12(\text{base})(\text{height})=\tfrac12 ab\sin C=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\ \ (\text{Heron})\] \[\Delta=\frac{abc}{4R}=rs=\tfrac12\left|x_1(y_2-y_3)+x_2(y_3-y_1)+x_3(y_1-y_2)\right|\]
  • From the three medians \(m_1,m_2,m_3\): \(\Delta=\frac43\times\) (area of the triangle whose sides are \(m_1,m_2,m_3\)).
  • Two triangles with a common angle: \(\dfrac{\Delta_1}{\Delta_2}=\dfrac{(\text{product of the two sides around that angle})_1}{(\text{same})_2}\).
Topper trick · Area ratios

Same base \(\Rightarrow\) area ratio = height ratio. Same height \(\Rightarrow\) area ratio = base ratio. This single idea cracks most area-ratio problems. If a cevian splits a side in ratio \(p:q\), it splits the triangle’s area in the same ratio \(p:q\) (equal heights).

1.4Right-angled triangles

  • Pythagoras: \(a^2+b^2=c^2\) (\(c\) = hypotenuse).
  • Altitude to the hypotenuse: \(h=\dfrac{ab}{c}\), and \(\dfrac1{h^2}=\dfrac1{a^2}+\dfrac1{b^2}\).
  • Median to hypotenuse \(=\dfrac c2=R\) (the circumcentre is the mid-point of the hypotenuse).
  • Inradius \(r=\dfrac{a+b-c}{2}=\dfrac{ab}{a+b+c}\).
Formula · Right triangle theorem

In \(\triangle ABC\) with \(\angle B=90^\circ\), draw the altitude \(BD\) to the hypotenuse \(AC\). Then \(\triangle ADB\sim\triangle BDC\sim\triangle ABC\), and with \(AD=p\), \(DC=q\), \(BD=h\):

\[BD^2=AD\cdot DC\ \ (h^2=pq)\qquad AB^2=AD\cdot AC\qquad BC^2=DC\cdot AC\]

So \(AB^2:BC^2=p:q\). With \(AD=8\) and \(DC=2\): \(BD=4\), \(AB=\sqrt{80}\), \(BC=\sqrt{20}\).

Topper trick · Square inside a right triangle

Legs \(a, b\), hypotenuse \(c\), altitude to the hypotenuse \(h\).

Square in the right-angle corner (two sides on the legs): side \(=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}\). Square standing on the hypotenuse: side \(=\dfrac{ch}{c+h}\).

Both follow from similar triangles. For 6-8-10: \(\frac{24}{7}\) in the corner, \(\frac{120}{37}\) on the hypotenuse.

1.5Equilateral triangle (side a)

Formula · Everything about the equilateral triangle
\[\Delta=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}a^2,\quad h=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}a,\quad r=\frac{a}{2\sqrt3}=\frac h3,\quad R=\frac{a}{\sqrt3}=\frac{2h}{3},\quad R=2r\]

Centroid, incentre, circumcentre and orthocentre coincide. Incircle area : circumcircle area \(=1:4\).

Topper trick · Viviani

For any interior point of an equilateral triangle, the sum of the perpendicular distances to the three sides equals the altitude \(h\), wherever the point is.

1.6Similarity & congruence

  • Similarity: AA, SAS, SSS. Congruence: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.
  • In similar triangles with ratio \(k\), all lengths (sides, medians, altitudes, bisectors, \(r\), \(R\), perimeter) scale by \(k\); areas scale by \(k^2\).
  • BPT (Thales): a line parallel to one side cuts the other two proportionally.
  • Mid-point theorem: the segment joining the mid-points of two sides is parallel to, and half of, the third side.
Exam trap · Ratio of areas

Sides in ratio \(2:3\) does not mean areas in ratio \(2:3\); it is \(4:9\). And two triangles with equal area are not necessarily congruent.

1.7Cevians: medians, bisectors, altitudes

Formula · Apollonius theorem

If \(AD\) is the median to \(BC\) (\(D\) the mid-point of \(BC\)):

\[AB^2+AC^2=2\left(AD^2+BD^2\right)\]

Example: \(AB=6\), \(AC=8\), \(BC=10\) gives \(36+64=2(AD^2+25)\), so \(AD=5\).

Formula · Median facts
\[m_a=\tfrac12\sqrt{2b^2+2c^2-a^2}\qquad\text{(Apollonius in side form)}\]

The three medians meet at the centroid, which divides each median \(2:1\) from the vertex, and they cut the triangle into 6 triangles of equal area.

Formula · Angle-bisector facts

The internal bisector from \(A\) divides \(BC\) as \(\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}=\dfrac cb\).

\[t_a=\frac{2bc\cos(A/2)}{b+c},\qquad t_a^2=bc-BD\cdot DC\]
Formula · Stewart’s theorem

Length of any cevian \(AD\), with \(BD=m\), \(DC=n\):

\[AB^2\cdot n+AC^2\cdot m=BC\left(AD^2+mn\right)\]

Each side is multiplied by the segment at the far end of \(BC\).

Formula · Exterior angle bisector

The bisector of the exterior angle at \(A\) meets \(BC\) produced at \(D\) with \(\dfrac{BD}{DC}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\) (external division).

Topper trick · Perpendicular medians

If the medians from \(B\) and \(C\) are perpendicular, then \(b^2+c^2=5a^2\) and \(\Delta=\tfrac23\,m_b\,m_c\).

Medians of 8 and 12 at right angles give an area of 64.

\(O, G, H\) are collinear with \(OG:GH=1:2\). \(OI^2=R^2-2Rr\) (Euler) \(\Rightarrow R\ge 2r\), with equality only for the equilateral triangle.

Topper trick · Incircle contact lengths

Where the incircle touches the sides, the tangent lengths from the vertices are \(s-a,\ s-b,\ s-c\). So if the incircle touches \(BC\) at \(X\), then \(BX=s-b\) and \(CX=s-c\). Deadly for “find the segment” problems.

Exradii: \(r_a=\dfrac{\Delta}{s-a}\), and \(\dfrac1r=\dfrac1{r_a}+\dfrac1{r_b}+\dfrac1{r_c}\).

Formula · Bisector angle results

Internal bisectors of \(B\) and \(C\) meet at \(I\): \(\angle BIC=90^\circ+\frac A2\).

Bisectors of the exterior angles at \(B\) and \(C\) meet at the excentre: angle there \(=90^\circ-\frac A2\).

Internal bisector of \(B\) and exterior bisector of \(C\) meet at \(E\): \(\angle BEC=\frac A2\).

1.9Concurrency: Ceva & Menelaus

  • Ceva (cevians \(AD, BE, CF\) concurrent): \(\dfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\dfrac{CE}{EA}\cdot\dfrac{AF}{FB}=1\).
  • Menelaus (points \(D, E, F\) collinear on the sides or a transversal): \(\dfrac{BD}{DC}\cdot\dfrac{CE}{EA}\cdot\dfrac{AF}{FB}=1\) (unsigned).

1.10Law of cosines shortcuts

Formula · Memorise these two

Included angle \(60^\circ\): \(a^2=b^2+c^2-bc\). Included angle \(120^\circ\): \(a^2=b^2+c^2+bc\).

2.2Core identities

Formula · Pythagorean identities
\[\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1,\qquad 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta,\qquad 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta\]
  • Sign rule (ASTC): Q1 All positive, Q2 Sin positive, Q3 Tan positive, Q4 Cos positive.
  • Complementary angles: \(\sin(90^\circ-\theta)=\cos\theta\), \(\tan(90^\circ-\theta)=\cot\theta\), and so on.

2.3Compound, multiple & half angles

Formula · Compound angles
\[\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B\pm\cos A\sin B\qquad \cos(A\pm B)=\cos A\cos B\mp\sin A\sin B\] \[\tan(A\pm B)=\frac{\tan A\pm\tan B}{1\mp\tan A\tan B}\]
Formula · Multiple angles
\[\sin2A=2\sin A\cos A=\frac{2\tan A}{1+\tan^2A}\] \[\cos2A=1-2\sin^2A=2\cos^2A-1=\frac{1-\tan^2A}{1+\tan^2A}\] \[\sin3A=3\sin A-4\sin^3A,\qquad \cos3A=4\cos^3A-3\cos A\]

2.4Triangle laws (used constantly in CAT & IPMAT)

Formula · Sine, cosine, projection
\[\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}=2R\qquad \cos C=\frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}\] \[a=b\cos C+c\cos B\qquad \Delta=\tfrac12 ab\sin C=2R^2\sin A\sin B\sin C\]

2.5Heights & distances

  • The angle of elevation is measured up from the horizontal; the angle of depression, down. Between the same two points they are equal (alternate angles).
  • Tower of height \(h\) seen from two collinear points a horizontal distance \(d\) apart, with elevations \(\alpha\) (nearer) and \(\beta\) (farther, \(\beta\lt\alpha\)): \(h=\dfrac{d}{\cot\beta-\cot\alpha}\).
Formula · Maximum & minimum

\(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\) lies in \(\left[-\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\ \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\right]\). \(\ \sin\theta\cos\theta\le\frac12\) (as \(\sin2\theta\le1\)).

Formula · Identities IPMAT likes
\[\sin C+\sin D=2\sin\tfrac{C+D}{2}\cos\tfrac{C-D}{2}\qquad \cos C+\cos D=2\cos\tfrac{C+D}{2}\cos\tfrac{C-D}{2}\] \[\cos C-\cos D=-2\sin\tfrac{C+D}{2}\sin\tfrac{C-D}{2}\qquad 2\sin A\cos B=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\]

If \(A+B+C=180^\circ\): \(\tan A+\tan B+\tan C=\tan A\tan B\tan C\).

If \(A+B=45^\circ\): \((1+\tan A)(1+\tan B)=2\).

Topper trick · More maximum & minimum results

\(a\sin^2\theta+b\cos^2\theta\) lies between \(a\) and \(b\).

For \(a,b\gt0\): \(a\tan^2\theta+b\cot^2\theta\ge2\sqrt{ab}\) and \(a\sin^2\theta+b\csc^2\theta\ge2\sqrt{ab}\) when \(b\le a\); otherwise its minimum is \(a+b\) (at \(\sin^2\theta=1\)). Both come from AM ≥ GM.

Topper trick · What the exams actually ask

Trigonometry mostly appears as (i) heights and distances and (ii) a stray identity or max–min question. Nail the standard-value table, the sine and cosine rules, and \(\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) for \(a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\); that covers about 90% of what is asked.

Quadrilaterals & Polygons

3.1General quadrilateral

  • Sum of interior angles \(=360^\circ\).
  • Area with diagonals \(d_1, d_2\) and angle \(\theta\) between them: \(\Delta=\tfrac12 d_1d_2\sin\theta\).
Topper trick · Varignon

Joining the mid-points of the sides of any quadrilateral gives a parallelogram whose area is exactly half the original.

3.2Cyclic quadrilateral (vertices on a circle)

Formula · Cyclic quadrilateral

Opposite angles are supplementary: \(A+C=B+D=180^\circ\).

Ptolemy: product of diagonals = sum of products of opposite sides: \(AC\cdot BD=AB\cdot CD+AD\cdot BC\).

Brahmagupta: \(\Delta=\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\), \(s=\dfrac{a+b+c+d}{2}\).

Topper trick · Largest area

For fixed side lengths, the quadrilateral of maximum area is the cyclic one. Whenever a question asks for the “largest area with these four sides”, use Brahmagupta directly.

3.3Parallelogram (sides a, b; diagonals d₁, d₂)

  • Opposite sides and angles equal; consecutive angles supplementary; diagonals bisect each other.
  • Area \(=\) base \(\times\) height \(=ab\sin\theta\) (\(\theta\) = included angle) \(=\tfrac12 d_1d_2\sin\varphi\) (\(\varphi\) = angle between diagonals).
  • Each diagonal halves it into congruent triangles; the two diagonals cut it into 4 equal-area triangles.
Formula · Parallelogram law
\[d_1^2+d_2^2=2\left(a^2+b^2\right)\]

The sum of the squares of the diagonals equals twice the sum of the squares of the sides.

Exam trap · Which figure is it?

A rhombus has equal sides but its diagonals are not equal (that would make it a square). A rectangle has equal diagonals but they are not perpendicular (again, that is a square). Only the square has both.

3.5Trapezium (parallel sides a, b; height h)

Formula · Trapezium

\(\Delta=\tfrac12(a+b)h\). Mid-segment (median) \(=\dfrac{a+b}{2}\), parallel to the bases.

  • The diagonals create 4 triangles; the two on the parallel sides are similar with ratio \(a:b\), and the two lateral triangles have equal area. The four areas are in the ratio \(a^2:ab:b^2:ab\).
  • A line \(EF\) parallel to the bases cuts the legs at \(E\) and \(F\) with \(AE:ED=m:n\) (top base \(AB=a\), bottom base \(CD=b\)): \(EF=\dfrac{m\,b+n\,a}{m+n}\).
  • Segment through the intersection of the diagonals, parallel to the bases \(=\dfrac{2ab}{a+b}\) (harmonic mean).
  • Segment parallel to the bases that bisects the area \(=\sqrt{\dfrac{a^2+b^2}{2}}\) (root-mean-square).
  • Isosceles trapezium: legs equal, diagonals equal, and it is cyclic.

3.6Regular polygon (n sides, side a)

Formula · Regular polygon

Topper trick · The hexagon

A regular hexagon is six equilateral triangles of side \(a\). So Area \(=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}a^2\), circumradius \(=a\), longer diagonal \(=2a\), shorter diagonal \(=a\sqrt3\), and it has 9 diagonals. This decomposition is the single biggest hexagon shortcut.

The triangle on alternate vertices is half the hexagon. The hexagon formed by joining the mid-points of the sides is \(\frac34\) of it.

Regular octagon: the square on alternate vertices has area \(1:\sqrt2\) of the octagon.

Topper trick · Counting inside a convex n-gon

With no three diagonals concurrent, the number of interior intersection points of diagonals \(=\binom n4\). The number of triangles formed by \(n\) points with no 3 collinear \(=\binom n3\).

Topper trick · Stars and staircase polygons

The five tip angles of a five-pointed star add to \(180^\circ\).

In a polygon whose sides are all horizontal or vertical, (corners of \(90^\circ\)) \(-\) (corners of \(270^\circ\)) \(=4\). So 25 convex corners means 21 concave ones.

Circles

4.1Basics

Formula · Circle, arc, sector, segment
\[\text{Circumference}=2\pi r=\pi d,\quad \text{Area}=\pi r^2,\quad \text{Annulus}=\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)\] \[\text{Arc}=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}2\pi r=r\theta_{\text{rad}},\quad \text{Sector}=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2=\tfrac12r^2\theta_{\text{rad}},\quad \text{Segment}=\tfrac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)_{\text{rad}}\]

4.2Chords

  • The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord; conversely, the line from the centre to the mid-point of a chord is perpendicular to it.
  • Equal chords are equidistant from the centre, cut off equal arcs and subtend equal angles at the centre. Conversely, chords equidistant from the centre are equal.
  • Chord at distance \(d\) from the centre: length \(=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}\). Chord subtending angle \(\theta\) at the centre \(=2r\sin\frac\theta2\).

4.3Angles in a circle

Formula · Angle theorems

Angle at centre \(=2\times\) angle at circumference on the same arc.

Angles in the same segment are equal. Angle in a semicircle \(=90^\circ\) (Thales).

Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

4.4Tangents & power of a point

  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. Two tangents from an external point are equal.
  • Length of the tangent from an external point at distance \(d\) from the centre \(=\sqrt{d^2-r^2}\).
Formula · Power of a point: one tool, three cases

Two chords meeting inside at \(P\): \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\).

Two secants from an external \(P\): \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\).

Tangent and secant from an external \(P\): \(PT^2=PA\cdot PB\).

Direct (external) common tangent \(=\sqrt{d^2-(R-r)^2}\). Transverse (internal) common tangent \(=\sqrt{d^2-(R+r)^2}\).

Topper trick · Rolling coin

A coin rolling without slipping around a fixed coin of the same size makes 2 full rotations, not 1. Around a circle of radius \(R\) (coin radius \(\rho\)), it makes \(\dfrac R\rho+1\) rotations externally.

5.2Cube & cuboid extras

  • Cube: sum of all edges \(=12a\). Sphere inscribed in a cube of side \(a\) has radius \(\frac a2\); the sphere through its vertices has radius \(\frac{a\sqrt3}{2}\).
  • Largest cube inside a sphere of radius \(R\): body diagonal \(=2R\Rightarrow\) side \(=\dfrac{2R}{\sqrt3}\).
  • Largest sphere inside a prism of height \(h\): radius \(=\) the smaller of the base’s inradius and \(\frac h2\).
Topper trick · Shortest path on a surface

Unfold the surface flat and join the two points with a straight line.

Cube of side \(a\), corner to opposite corner: \(\sqrt{(2a)^2+a^2}=a\sqrt5\). Cylinder, a point on the bottom rim to the opposite point on the top rim: \(\sqrt{h^2+(\pi r)^2}\).

5.3Regular tetrahedron (edge a) & Euler’s formula

Formula · Regular tetrahedron
\[V=\frac{\sqrt2}{12}a^3=\frac{a^3}{6\sqrt2},\quad SA=\sqrt3\,a^2,\quad h=\frac{a\sqrt6}{3},\quad R=\frac{a\sqrt6}{4},\quad r=\frac{a\sqrt6}{12}\ (R=3r)\]
Formula · Euler’s polyhedron formula

\(V-E+F=2\). Cube: \(8-12+6=2\); tetrahedron: \(4-6+4=2\).

5.4Hollow solids, rolled sheets & water levels

Formula · Hollow solids

Hollow cylinder (outer radius \(R\), inner \(r\), height \(h\)): volume \(=\pi h(R^2-r^2)\); total surface \(=2\pi h(R+r)+2\pi(R^2-r^2)\).

Spherical shell: volume \(=\tfrac43\pi(R^3-r^3)\). Hollow hemisphere (bowl): total surface \(=2\pi R^2+2\pi r^2+\pi(R^2-r^2)\).

Formula · Rolling a sector into a cone

A sector of radius \(L\) and angle \(\theta\) becomes a cone with slant height \(l=L\) and base radius \(r=\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}L\) (arc length \(=2\pi r\)).

The cone’s curved surface equals the sector’s area: \(\pi rl\).

Topper trick · Rolling a rectangle into a cylinder

An \(l\times b\) sheet rolled so that \(l\) becomes the circumference: \(r=\dfrac{l}{2\pi}\), \(h=b\), volume \(=\dfrac{l^2b}{4\pi}\).

Rolling it the other way gives volume \(\dfrac{b^2l}{4\pi}\). The curved surface is \(lb\) both ways, but the volumes are in the ratio \(l:b\).

Formula · Water-level rise

Rise in level \(=\dfrac{\text{volume of the object immersed}}{\text{base area of the vessel}}\).

A sphere of radius 5 dropped into a cylinder of radius 10: \(\frac{\frac43\pi\cdot125}{100\pi}=\frac53\).

Exam trap · Open or closed?

Read whether the vessel is open (no top), hollow (inner surface counts) or a solid. Most surface-area mistakes come from adding or dropping a lid or the inner surface.

5.5The painted cube cut into n³ small cubes

Formula · Painted-cube counts
3 faces painted (corners)\(8\) (always)
2 faces painted (edges)\(12(n-2)\)
1 face painted (face centres)\(6(n-2)^2\)
0 faces painted (interior)\((n-2)^3\)

Check: \(8+12(n-2)+6(n-2)^2+(n-2)^3=n^3\).

Try itPainted cube
Topper trick · Scaling & recasting

Scaling law. If every length of a solid is multiplied by \(k\), surface areas multiply by \(k^2\) and volumes by \(k^3\).

Recasting. When a solid is melted and reshaped, volume is conserved, so just equate the two volumes. These two ideas dominate CAT and IPMAT mensuration.

Topper trick · Cutting a cone

A cut parallel to the base leaves a small cone similar to the whole: \(\dfrac hH=\dfrac rR=\dfrac lL\), so its volume is \(\left(\dfrac hH\right)^3\) of the whole.

Cut a 24 cm cone 4 cm above the base: the top is \((20/24)^3=\frac{125}{216}\), so the two parts are \(125:91\). The same idea answers water-filling questions on conical vessels.

Exam trap · Doubling the radius

Doubling the radius of a sphere multiplies its surface area by 4 and its volume by 8, not by 2. Always ask whether the ratio asked for is linear, area or volume.

Miscellaneous

6.1Coordinate geometry

Formula · Points & lines
\[\text{Distance}=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\qquad \text{Mid-point}=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\] \[\text{Section } (m:n,\text{ internal})=\left(\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n}\right)\qquad \text{Centroid}=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3},\frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)\] \[m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\tan\theta;\quad \text{parallel: } m_1=m_2;\quad \text{perpendicular: } m_1m_2=-1;\quad \tan\theta=\left|\frac{m_1-m_2}{1+m_1m_2}\right|\] \[\text{Point to line } ax+by+c=0:\ \frac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}};\qquad \text{between parallels: } \frac{|c_1-c_2|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\]
Formula · More point results
\[\text{Section } (m:n,\text{ external})=\left(\frac{mx_2-nx_1}{m-n},\frac{my_2-ny_1}{m-n}\right)\qquad \text{Incentre}=\left(\frac{ax_1+bx_2+cx_3}{a+b+c},\frac{ay_1+by_2+cy_3}{a+b+c}\right)\]

Parallelogram \(ABCD\): the diagonals share a mid-point, so \(x_1+x_3=x_2+x_4\) and \(y_1+y_3=y_2+y_4\).

Quadrant signs: I \((+,+)\), II \((-,+)\), III \((-,-)\), IV \((+,-)\).

Formula · Equation of a line

Point-slope: \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\). Two-point: \(y-y_1=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}(x-x_1)\). Slope-intercept: \(y=mx+c\). Intercept form: \(\dfrac xa+\dfrac yb=1\).

From \(ax+by+c=0\): slope \(=-\dfrac ab\), \(x\)-intercept \(=-\dfrac ca\), \(y\)-intercept \(=-\dfrac cb\), distance from the origin \(=\dfrac{|c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\).

Parallel to the \(x\)-axis: \(y=b\) (slope 0). Parallel to the \(y\)-axis: \(x=a\) (slope undefined).

Formula · Collinearity, foot, reflection

Three points are collinear when the triangle they form has zero area (or the slopes \(AB\) and \(BC\) are equal).

For the line \(ax+by+c=0\) and the point \((x_0,y_0)\), let \(k=\dfrac{ax_0+by_0+c}{a^2+b^2}\). Foot of the perpendicular \(=(x_0-ak,\ y_0-bk)\). Reflection \(=(x_0-2ak,\ y_0-2bk)\).

The line \(ax+by+c=0\) divides the join of \(P_1, P_2\) in the ratio \(-\dfrac{ax_1+by_1+c}{ax_2+by_2+c}\) (positive means internally).

Quadrilateral \(ABCD\) (vertices in order): area \(=\tfrac12\left|(x_1y_2-x_2y_1)+(x_2y_3-x_3y_2)+(x_3y_4-x_4y_3)+(x_4y_1-x_1y_4)\right|\).

Topper trick · Locus questions

Call the moving point \((x,y)\), write the condition with the distance or slope formula, and simplify. \(PA=PB\) gives the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\); \(\angle APB=90^\circ\) gives the circle on \(AB\) as diameter.

Formula · Circle equations

\((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\). General form \(x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\Rightarrow\) centre \((-g,-f)\), radius \(\sqrt{g^2+f^2-c}\).

Topper trick · Shoelace & Pick

Polygon area from vertices \(=\tfrac12\left|\sum(x_iy_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\right|\). For a lattice polygon, Pick’s theorem: Area \(=I+\dfrac B2-1\) (\(I\) = interior, \(B\) = boundary lattice points).

6.2Locus & symmetry

  • Equidistant from two points \(\Rightarrow\) the perpendicular bisector.
  • Equidistant from two lines \(\Rightarrow\) the pair of angle bisectors.
  • Fixed distance from a point \(\Rightarrow\) a circle.

6.3Elite named results

Topper trick · Multi-step problems in one line

British flag theorem: for any point \(P\) and rectangle \(ABCD\), \(PA^2+PC^2=PB^2+PD^2\).

Viviani: for an interior point of an equilateral triangle, the sum of distances to the sides = altitude.

Median triangle: the three medians of a triangle can themselves form a triangle whose area is \(\frac34\) of the original.

Regions from lines: \(n\) lines in general position split the plane into \(1+n+\binom n2\) regions.

Chords & regions: \(n\) points on a circle, all chords drawn (no 3 concurrent) \(\Rightarrow 1+\binom n2+\binom n4\) regions.

1How many triangles with integer sides have perimeter 24?

Solution. n = 24 is even, so 24² ÷ 48 = 12.

2Find the area of a triangle with sides 13, 14 and 15.

Solution. s = 21. Area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84.

3Find the circumradius of the triangle with sides 13, 14 and 15.

Solution. R = abc ÷ 4Δ = (13 × 14 × 15) ÷ (4 × 84) = 2730 ÷ 336 = 8.125.

4In triangle ABC, AB = 13, BC = 14, CA = 15. The incircle touches BC at X. Find BX.

Solution. s = 21. BX = s − CA = 21 − 15 = 6.

5A right triangle has legs 9 and 40. Find its inradius.

Solution. Hypotenuse = 41 (9-40-41). r = (9 + 40 − 41) ÷ 2 = 4.

6How many diagonals does a regular 12-sided polygon have?

Solution. n(n − 3) ÷ 2 = 12 × 9 ÷ 2 = 54.

7In a convex octagon with no three diagonals concurrent, how many points of intersection do the diagonals have inside it?

Solution. Every 4 vertices give one crossing: ⁸C₄ = 70.

8What is each interior angle of a regular decagon, in degrees?

Solution. (n − 2) × 180 ÷ n = 8 × 180 ÷ 10 = 144.

9A trapezium has parallel sides 1 and 7. Find the length of the segment parallel to them that divides its area into two equal parts.

Solution. Equal-area cut = √((1² + 7²) ÷ 2) = √25 = 5.

10A point is 10 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 6 cm. Find the length of the tangent from the point.

Solution. Tangent = √(d² − r²) = √(100 − 36) = 8.

11Two circles of radii 8 and 3 have centres 13 apart. Find the length of a direct common tangent.

Solution. Direct tangent = √(d² − (R − r)²) = √(169 − 25) = 12.

12A coin of radius 1 rolls without slipping once around the outside of a fixed circle of radius 3. How many rotations does it make?

Solution. Rotations = R ÷ ρ + 1 = 3 ÷ 1 + 1 = 4.

13A painted cube is cut into 6 × 6 × 6 small cubes. How many small cubes have exactly two faces painted?

Solution. Edge cubes = 12(n − 2) = 12 × 4 = 48.

14The radius of a sphere is doubled. By what factor does its volume increase?

Solution. Volume ∝ r³, so 2³ = 8.

15Into how many regions do 10 lines in general position divide the plane?

Solution. 1 + n + ⁿC₂ = 1 + 10 + 45 = 56.

16How many obtuse-angled triangles have integer sides 8, 15 and x?

Solution. x longest: x² > 64 + 225 = 289, and x < 23, so x = 18 to 22 (5). 15 longest: x² < 225 − 64 = 161, and x > 7, so x = 8 to 12 (5). Total 10.

17In triangle ABC, the medians BP = 8 and CQ = 12 are perpendicular to each other. Find the area of ABC.

Solution. Area = ⅔ × 8 × 12 = 64.

18A triangle has sides 15, 20 and 25. A square has two vertices on the side of length 25 and one vertex on each of the other two sides. Find the side of the square.

Solution. It is a right triangle; altitude on 25 is h = 15 × 20 ÷ 25 = 12. Side = ch ÷ (c + h) = 300 ÷ 37 ≈ 8.11.

19Every side of a polygon is parallel to the x-axis or the y-axis. It has 25 corners of 90°. How many corners of 270° does it have?

Solution. Convex − concave = 4, so concave = 25 − 4 = 21.

20Find the distance of the point (2, 1) from the line 4x + 3y − 7 = 0.

Solution. |4(2) + 3(1) − 7| ÷ √(4² + 3²) = 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8.

CAT / IPMATChallenge set

10 questions at CAT / IPMAT level, each combining two or three ideas from the handbook. Give each one about 2 minutes before you look at the solution.

C1A right triangle has sides 6, 8 and 10. Find OI², where O is its circumcentre and I its incentre.

Solution. R = 5 and r = (6 + 8 − 10) ÷ 2 = 2. Euler: OI² = R² − 2Rr = 25 − 20 = 5.

C2Two circles of radii 9 and 4 touch externally. A third circle touches both of them and their direct common tangent, and sits between them. Find its radius.

Solution. For circles touching each other and a common line, 1/√r = 1/√9 + 1/√4 = 1/3 + 1/2 = 5/6. So √r = 6/5 and r = 36/25 = 1.44.

C3ABCD is a square of side 10. E and F are the mid-points of AB and BC. AF and DE meet at P. Find the area of triangle APE.

Solution. Put A = (0, 0), B = (10, 0), D = (0, 10). AF: y = x/2. DE: y = 10 − 2x. They meet at P = (4, 2). Triangle APE has base AE = 5 and height 2, so its area is 5. (AF ⟂ DE here, a useful fact in its own right.)

C4In triangle ABC of area 90, points D, E, F lie on BC, CA, AB with BD : DC = CE : EA = AF : FB = 1 : 2. Find the area of triangle DEF.

Solution. Each corner triangle shares an angle with ABC: area AFE = (1/3)(2/3) = 2/9 of ABC, and likewise BDF and CED. DEF = 1 − 3 × 2/9 = 1/3 of 90 = 30.

C5A sector of radius 21 cm and angle 120° is rolled into a cone. Find the square of the cone’s height (in cm²).

Solution. Slant l = 21. Arc = (120/360) × 2π × 21 = 14π = 2πr, so r = 7. h² = 21² − 7² = 441 − 49 = 392.

C6A quadrilateral has sides 4, 5, 7 and 10. What is the largest area it can have?

Solution. The largest area comes when it is cyclic. s = 13. Brahmagupta: √(9 × 8 × 6 × 3) = √1296 = 36.

C7The point (3, 4) is reflected in the line x + y = 1 to the point (a, b). Find a + b.

Solution. For x + y − 1 = 0: k = (3 + 4 − 1) ÷ 2 = 3. Reflection = (3 − 2·3, 4 − 2·3) = (−3, −2). So a + b = −5.

C8A sphere of radius 5 cm is dropped into a cylindrical vessel of radius 10 cm partly filled with water. By how many cm does the water rise?

Solution. Rise = sphere volume ÷ base area = (4/3)π(125) ÷ 100π = 5/3 ≈ 1.67 cm.

C9From two points 60 m apart on level ground, on the same side of a tower and in line with it, the angles of elevation of its top are 30° and 60°. Find h², where h is the tower’s height in m.

Solution. h = 60 ÷ (cot 30° − cot 60°) = 60 ÷ (√3 − 1/√3) = 60 ÷ (2/√3) = 30√3. h² = 2700.

C10How many distinct triangles with perimeter 44 have every side a multiple of 4?

Solution. Divide every side by 4: integer triangles with perimeter 11. Odd n, so (11 + 3)² ÷ 48 ≈ 4.08, i.e. 4: (1,5,5), (2,4,5), (3,3,5), (3,4,4).
